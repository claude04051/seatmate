# Trend, Support/Resistance & Market Structure

The foundation everything else (patterns, indicators, volume) gets layered on top of.
Get this read right first — a "bullish pattern" against a broken downtrend structure
is a much weaker read than the same pattern inside an established uptrend.

## Market structure

- **Uptrend**: a sequence of higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL). The trend is
  intact as long as each pullback holds above the prior low; a lower low breaks
  the structure and is the first objective sign of a possible trend change.
- **Downtrend**: mirror image — lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
- **Range/consolidation**: no clear sequence of higher/lower highs and lows — price
  oscillating between a ceiling and floor. Ranges resolve eventually; trading the
  boundaries (buy support, sell resistance) is the standard approach until a
  genuine breakout occurs.
- Structure should be read on the timeframe relevant to the user's trading style —
  a chart can be in a higher-timeframe uptrend while pulling back in a lower-
  timeframe downtrend simultaneously; state which timeframe the structure read
  applies to.

## Trendlines

- Drawn by connecting two or more swing highs (downtrend line) or swing lows
  (uptrend line). The more times a trendline is touched and respected, the more
  significant it's considered — but also the more "telegraphed," since everyone
  watching the same chart sees the same line.
- A trendline break is more meaningful with a *closing* break (not just an
  intraday wick through it) and, ideally, above-average volume.
- Steep trendlines (very sharp angle) are inherently less reliable/sustainable
  than shallower ones — a nearly vertical move is by definition unsustainable and
  likely to break its trendline soon even if the broader trend continues at a
  calmer pace afterward.

## Support and resistance

- **Definition**: price levels where buying (support) or selling (resistance)
  pressure has historically been strong enough to stall or reverse price. Often
  simply prior swing highs/lows, but also round numbers, prior gap levels, and
  high-volume nodes (see `references/volume-analysis.md`).
- **Role reversal**: a broken resistance level frequently becomes support on a
  retest (and vice versa for broken support becoming resistance) — this is one of
  the most reliable, testable pieces of classical TA and a common lower-risk
  entry: wait for the breakout, then wait for the retest of the broken level
  holding, rather than chasing the initial breakout candle.
- **Strength of a level** scales with: how many times it's been tested, how much
  volume traded there (see Volume Profile in `references/volume-analysis.md`),
  and how recently it was established (more recent = more relevant to current
  participants).
- Levels are zones, not exact prices — especially on volatile names, treat a
  level as a band (e.g. "resistance around 42.00-42.50") rather than a single
  tick, and don't over-index on a wick piercing a level by a few cents.

## Fibonacci retracement

- Drawn from a swing low to swing high (uptrend pullback) or swing high to swing
  low (downtrend bounce). Key levels: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%.
- **How to use it**: identifies *where* a pullback might find support (in an
  uptrend) or resistance (in a downtrend) before the prior trend resumes. The
  38.2%-61.8% zone ("golden zone") is the most commonly watched area for a
  pullback to hold if the trend is going to continue.
- **Caveat**: Fibonacci levels work best as *confluence* — a fib level that lines
  up with a prior swing high/low, a moving average, or a volume node is a much
  stronger level than a bare fib level with nothing else there. Don't present a
  fib level in isolation as if it has independent predictive power.
- **Extensions** (127.2%, 161.8%, etc.) are the equivalent tool for projecting
  potential targets *beyond* the prior swing, once a move is confirmed continuing.

## Putting structure together with the rest of the read

Order of operations for a full read: identify the trend/structure first (this
sets the directional bias), then look for patterns and levels *within* that
structure, then confirm with volume and indicators. A pattern that aligns with
the higher-timeframe trend (e.g. a bull flag inside an uptrend, a cup and handle
breaking a downtrend structure) is a materially stronger setup than the identical
shape fighting the prevailing trend.
